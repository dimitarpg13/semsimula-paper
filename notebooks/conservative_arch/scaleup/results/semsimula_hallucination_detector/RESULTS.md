## 3. Key findings

### 3.1 The "domain-flip" effect (main finding)

For **TinyStories-trained** models, `H_softmax` is the dominant signal (AUROC 0.585–0.628),
comfortably above chance, while `ΔE_anomaly` is above chance but weaker (0.449–0.554).

For **OpenWebText-trained** models the picture **inverts**:
- `H_softmax` drops to near chance (0.465–0.488)
- `ΔE_anomaly` becomes the strongest single signal (0.534–0.539)

This is the paper's central prediction: entropy-based detection works only when the model
assigns high confidence to in-distribution continuations. On OpenWebText, both clean and
corrupted continuations are broadly fluent, so next-token entropy provides little separation.
The energy-dissipation signal `ΔE_anomaly`, by contrast, captures whether the *attractor
basin* that the hidden state sits in is consistent with the context — a structural property
that does not depend on surface fluency.

### 3.2 Phase 3 vs Phase 2 (OWT)

| Signal | Phase 2 | Phase 3 | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ΔE_anomaly | 0.534 | 0.539 | **+0.005** |
| K_max | 0.507 | 0.464 | −0.043 |
| H_softmax | 0.488 | 0.465 | −0.023 |
| [ΔE, K_max] | 0.532 | **0.543** | +0.011 |
| [ΔE, K_max, H] | 0.515 | 0.536 | **+0.021** |

Phase 3 improves `ΔE_anomaly` marginally (+0.005) and the combined detectors more
substantially (+0.011 to +0.021). The degradation in `K_max` (−0.043) is explained by
the Phase 3 γ_eff analysis below.

### 3.3 Effective damping γ_eff (T-ratio measurement)

| Model | γ_param | γ_eff | Reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Multi-Xi SPLM (TS) | 0.300 | **0.2725** | −9% |
| Fock v2.1 (TS) | 0.300 | 0.1637 | −45% |
| Fock Attention (TS) | 0.300 | 0.1111 | −63% |
| OWT Multi-Xi SPLM (P2) | 0.300 | **0.0283** | −91% |
| OWT Scaled SPLM (P3) | 0.300 | **0.1108** | −63% |

**OWT Phase 2** (γ_eff = 0.028) is almost fully counter-damped by LayerNorm — the
nominal γ is nearly negated. **OWT Phase 3** recovers to γ_eff = 0.111, matching Fock
Attention — the deeper V_theta MLP creates potential wells strong enough to resist LN
counter-damping more effectively.

The K_max degradation in Phase 3 (−0.043) is consistent with this recovery: with more
residual damping, the energy landscape has shallower transient curvature spikes in the
continuation window, making the spectral curvature proxy less sensitive. Meanwhile
`ΔE_anomaly` — which measures *integrated* energy shift — benefits from the sharper
basin structure of the deeper V_theta.

### 3.4 Mean kinetic energy T_mean (layer profile)

| Model | T_mean[0] | T_mean[last] | Range |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| OWT Multi-Xi SPLM (P2) | 24.8 | 18.7 | low, flat |
| OWT Scaled SPLM (P3) | **179.8** | 19.3 | high initial, decays |

Phase 3 has ~7× higher kinetic energy in layer 0, confirming significantly deeper
potential wells. The fast decay across layers shows the deeper V_theta rapidly
dissipates the initial high-energy state into a more structured attractor — which
is what generates the improved `ΔE_anomaly` separation.

## 5. Interpretation and next steps

The results confirm the **qualitative correctness** of the energy-based detection
hypothesis: on a richer training corpus (OWT), entropy becomes uninformative while
`ΔE_anomaly` rises to become the best single signal. The absolute AUROC values
(0.534–0.543) remain modest, and further improvements could come from:

1. **Longer training / larger model**: Phase 3 improved `ΔE_anomaly` by +0.005 over
   Phase 2 with 2× V_theta capacity and 2× steps. Continued scale-up may widen the gap.
2. **Longer continuation windows**: `cont_len=32` tokens is short; averaging over longer
   continuations should reduce signal variance.
3. **γ_eff re-calibration**: Phase 3's γ_eff=0.111 suggests the damping floor has room
   to be further tuned — a learned γ on OWT could better balance well depth vs counter-damping.
4. **Context-length scaling**: The multi-ξ channel hierarchy (α=[0, 0.447, 0.800, 0.984])
   suggests the model already encodes multi-scale context; longer prompts (>96 tokens)
   may sharpen ΔE_anomaly further.
