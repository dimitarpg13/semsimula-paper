## 1. Motivation

Across seven independent gamma sweeps spanning the Fock-PARFLM and SPLM families (`d` = 256 to 1024, MLP and anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$, TinyStories and OpenWebText), a **geodesic residual analysis** consistently recovers an intrinsic effective damping $\gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.9–0.98$ — largely independent of the explicit training-time damping $\gamma_{\text{train}}$, which ranges from 0.05 to 0.50 across these same sweeps. This raises an immediate question: if the realized dynamics is already heavily damped regardless of the explicit dial, does that mean the second-order machinery — the velocity buffer, the friction term, the whole inertial apparatus — is dispensable? Could a first-order gradient-flow Lagrangian have reached similar perplexities?

This document works through why that inference does not follow, disentangles two genuinely different hypotheses that are easy to conflate (does the model *behave* like a first-order system at inference, vs does *removing inertia during training* hurt), reviews the current evidentiary status of each, and proposes a new pre-registered ablation to close the gap for the architecture family (anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$ + Fock coupling regularisation) that the current scale-up work actually uses.

## 2. Two Damping Channels: Explicit vs Implicit

Fock-PARFLM's per-layer update (`model_parf_multixi.py`, `_layer_step`) is a damped velocity-Verlet step:

$$
\delta_\ell = h_\ell - h_{\ell-1}, \qquad f_\ell = -\nabla_h\big(V_\theta + U_{\text{pair}}\big),
$$

$$
h_{\ell+1} = \mathrm{LN}\left(h_\ell + \frac{\delta_\ell}{1+\Delta t \gamma} + \frac{\Delta t^2}{m_b(1+\Delta t \gamma)} f_\ell\right).
$$

The quantity $r_{\text{layer}} = 1/(1+\Delta t \gamma)$ is the **per-layer velocity retention factor**: the fraction of the previous step's displacement $\delta_\ell$ that survives into the next layer. $\gamma$ here is the single **explicit** friction coefficient exposed as a training hyperparameter ($\gamma_{\text{train}}$ in the sweep tables).

The geodesic residual pipeline (`Geodesic_Preservation_Experiment.md`) fits a *different* quantity: given an already-trained model's realized hidden-state trajectory, what value of $\gamma$ — plugged into the same damped-geodesic equation of motion — best explains the observed path? This recovered value, $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$, characterizes the model's **total effective damping**, from every source: the explicit $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ term, plus LayerNorm's radial re-projection at every layer, plus the curvature of the learned potential itself, plus the reverse channel and register dynamics. It is this total that governs how the trajectory actually behaves, not $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ in isolation.

The empirical finding is that $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ clusters tightly around 0.9–0.98 in **every** sweep run to date, regardless of $\gamma_{\text{train}}$, $d$, or $V_\theta$ family:

| Sweep | $d$ | $V_\theta$ | $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ range tested | $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ (mean, excl. instability outliers) | Implied $r_{\text{layer}}(\gamma_{\text{geo}})$ |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| d=256, TinyStories | 256 | aniso-Gaussian | 0.05–0.50 | ~0.965 | ~0.509 |
| d=384, OWT | 384 | MLP | 0.05–0.50 | ~0.935 | ~0.517 |
| d=384, OWT | 384 | aniso-Gaussian | 0.05–0.50 | ~0.975 | ~0.506 |
| d=768, OWT (old) | 768 | MLP | 0.05–0.40 | *(geodesic analysis not run — pending)* | — |
| d=768, OWT (new) | 768 | aniso-Gaussian | 0.05–0.50 | 0.981 (std 0.0035) | ~0.505 |
| d=1024, OWT | 1024 | isotropic Gaussian | 0.05–0.20 | ~0.933 | ~0.517 |
| d=1024, OWT (new) | 1024 | aniso-Gaussian | 0.05–0.50 | 0.963 (std 0.0030) | ~0.510 |

The last column is new here and worth dwelling on: converting every recovered $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ back into a per-layer retention factor gives **~0.50–0.52 across every architecture, scale, and corpus tested.** Roughly half the velocity survives each layer in the *realized* dynamics, essentially independent of what the explicit training dial was set to. That consistency is itself evidence that this is a genuine architectural invariant (dominated by LayerNorm's fixed-radius re-projection, which the companion notes already identify as the leading implicit-damping suspect) rather than noise.

![Two damping channels — explicit gamma_train and implicit LayerNorm/curvature damping — combine into the total effective damping recovered as gamma_geo, with per-layer velocity retention of roughly 51 percent regardless of the explicit dial](images/damping_channels_explicit_vs_implicit.png)

**Figure 1.** The explicit friction dial ($\gamma_{\text{train}}$, 0.05–0.50) and the implicit damping contributed by LayerNorm's radial re-projection and the potential's curvature are separate channels that sum into the total effective damping an already-trained model exhibits ($\gamma_{\text{geo}} \approx 0.9–0.98$). The bottom row illustrates why this still carries genuine cross-layer memory rather than reducing to a per-step force lookup: at the recovered $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$, roughly half the previous layer's displacement survives into the next layer at every step.

## 3. Why "Implicit Dominates" Does Not Imply First-Order Suffices

It is tempting to read the table above as: *the realized system is already so damped that a first-order reduction would do just as well.* Three specific reasons this does not follow.

### 3.1 Two different senses of "damped enough to look first-order" — and they disagree

Section 2's $r_{\text{layer}}(\gamma_{\text{geo}}) \approx 0.51$ is a *per-layer* number. Whether that counts as "close to overdamped" depends entirely on what it is compared against — and the two natural comparisons give opposite answers. (An earlier draft of this section made only the first comparison and drew the wrong overall conclusion from it; both comparisons are given here.)

**By local, single-step comparison:** the first-order gradient-flow limit (`Replacing_The_Conservative_Mechanism_of_SPLM_with_First_Order.md`, §3.1) is the regime $\gamma \gg \omega_0$ (friction much larger than the system's natural frequency), where the velocity term becomes negligible *at every step* and the equation of motion collapses to $\dot h = -\tilde\beta \nabla V_\theta$. That is a statement about $r_{\text{layer}} \to 0$ — velocity forgotten almost entirely, one step to the next. $r_{\text{layer}}(\gamma_{\text{geo}}) \approx 0.51$ is nowhere near that: roughly half of the *immediately preceding* layer's displacement is still carried forward at every single step.

**By the cumulative, full-stack comparison the Scale-Up doc itself uses:** `Fock-PARFLM_Scale-Up_Gamma_Sweep_Results_and_Damping_Regime_Analysis.md` does not classify a configuration as "overdamped" or "underdamped" using $r_{\text{layer}}$ in isolation — it uses the cumulative retention over the network's full depth, $r_{\text{total}} = r_{\text{layer}}^{L}$. That is exactly how it labels d=384's $\gamma_{\text{train}}=0.25$–$0.30$ ($r_{\text{total}} = 1.5$–$2.8\%$) **overdamped**, and d=768/d=1024's $\gamma_{\text{train}}=0.05$ ($r_{\text{total}} = 45.7$–$55.7\%$) **underdamped**. Applying that identical formula to $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ instead of $\gamma_{\text{train}}$, at the same depths, gives:

| Configuration | $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ | $L$ | $r_{\text{total}}$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| d=384 MLP | ~0.935 | 16 | ~0.0026% |
| d=384 aniso-Gaussian | ~0.975 | 16 | ~0.0019% |
| d=768 aniso-Gaussian | 0.981 | 16 | ~0.0018% |
| d=1024 isotropic | ~0.933 | 16 | ~0.0026% |
| d=1024 aniso-Gaussian | 0.963 | 16 | ~0.0021% |

By this convention, $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ implies a regime roughly **1,000x more overdamped** than the explicitly-labeled d=384 "overdamped" configuration — at every scale tested, including d=768 and d=1024, whose *training* dial sits in the "underdamped" regime. Judged by the project's own operational definition of the damping regime — cumulative decay over the network's actual depth, not a single layer's retention in isolation — the intrinsic, realized dynamics of every checkpoint analyzed to date are heavily overdamped, not moderately damped.

**Caveat:** $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ is fit against the continuum damped-geodesic ODE — an additive $\gamma v$ term — not literally the discrete Verlet formula $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ enters through, so translating it via $r_{\text{layer}}=1/(1+\gamma_{\text{geo}})$ is an extrapolation the geodesic-residual pipeline itself does not make explicitly. The qualitative conclusion — $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ implies far heavier cumulative damping than any $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ ever tested, whose maximum was 0.50 — is robust to this modeling choice, even though the exact percentages above should not be read as precisely as the paper's own reported 2.8%/55.7% figures.

**Neither comparison supersedes the other — they answer different questions.** The cumulative number says: by the time a token's representation reaches the final layer, almost none of its layer-0 velocity survives intact, so long-range momentum transport across the full stack is negligible. This is genuinely strong, independent corroboration of the inference-phase claim (§4.1) that trained models are observationally close to first-order at the resolution the Markov-order test probes. But the per-layer number says something the cumulative number cannot: at *every individual step*, roughly half of the *immediately preceding* layer's displacement is still live. Compounded over just 2–3 layers, that is still $0.51^2 \approx 26\%$ and $0.51^3 \approx 13\%$ of a *recent* (not layer-0) velocity contribution — a short-range inertial effect that a literal first-order recursion, which has zero retention at any range including the very next step, cannot replicate at all. Long-range memory being negligible does not mean short-range memory is; these are compatible, not contradictory, findings.

### 3.2 The first-order reduction collapses two knobs into one

The full second-order layer update independently controls a step-size-like quantity ($\Delta t^2/m_b$) and a friction coefficient ($\gamma$); the first-order reduction absorbs both into a single ratio $\tilde\beta = \beta/\gamma$. Even granting that a trained second-order model's trajectory can be *post-hoc* summarized by one $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ value, that says nothing about whether the *optimum* of the strictly-smaller, one-parameter first-order family reaches the same task performance. If the useful behaviour requires independently tuning "how big a step" and "how much cross-layer smoothing," a family that only has one combined knob cannot reach it by construction — no matter how the knob is set.

![Second order dynamics occupy a two dimensional friction versus step size plane while the first order reduction is confined to a one dimensional curve within it, illustrating that a trained second order optimum lying off that curve cannot be reached by any single first order step size choice](images/degrees_of_freedom_collapse_first_order_reduction.png)

**Figure 3.** The second-order family independently controls friction ($\gamma$) and step size ($\beta$); the first-order reduction fixes their ratio $\tilde\beta = \beta/\gamma$, confining it to a one-dimensional curve inside that plane. If a matched-compute second-order optimum sits off the curve, no choice of the single first-order knob reaches it — the gap illustrated here is the mechanism §6's Fock-G1 protocol is designed to measure directly rather than infer.

### 3.3 The gamma sweeps confound "higher $\gamma$" with "smaller effective step"

In every sweep reviewed here, $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ is varied while the learning-rate schedule is held fixed — $\tilde\beta = \beta/(1+\Delta t \gamma)$ is *not* held constant across the sweep. As $\gamma$ increases, the implied effective step size mechanically shrinks. So "larger $\gamma$ performs worse" in these sweeps is at least partly an "effective step size became too small" artifact, not necessarily a demonstration that a **properly, independently re-tuned** first-order model would also underperform. This is exactly the confound a fair first-order-vs-second-order test has to control for (§6.3 addresses it directly).

### 3.4 Standard transformers have no depth-axis velocity coordinate to compound

§3.1's two comparisons — per-layer $r_{\text{layer}}\approx0.51$ vs. cumulative $r_{\text{total}}\approx0.002\%$ — are both statements about Fock-PARFLM/SPLM's *depth* axis, where $(h_\ell, v_\ell)$ is a genuine $2n$-dimensional phase space at every layer, by construction (§2). It is worth being explicit that the entire compounding story — $r_{\text{layer}}$, $r_{\text{total}}$, $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ — presupposes an architecture with a real depth-axis velocity coordinate to begin with. Standard attention transformers do not have one, and this is a difference of kind, not degree.

Under the Lu et al. (2020) layer-as-time reading the paper itself adopts as one of the two first-order shallow limits of the full Euler–Lagrange equation (`Evidence_for_second_order_ODE_governing_evolution.md`, "Lu et al. — First-Order in Layer Depth"), a standard transformer block is $\dot x_i = F_{\text{conv}}(x_i) + F_{\text{diff}}(x_i, \{x_j\}_{j\neq i})$ — one Euler step of a first-order convection–diffusion flow per layer, phase space dimension $n$, not $2n$. There is no separate momentum variable living alongside $h_\ell$ across depth at all, so there is nothing for a compounding-retention story to be told about on that axis in that architecture. Fock-PARFLM/SPLM's $\approx99.998\%$ cumulative long-range damping (§3.1) is the result of a real per-layer retention factor ($r_{\text{layer}}\approx0.51$, never zero) being multiplied by itself $L$ times; a standard transformer's depth axis was never allocated the coordinate whose retention could be measured in the first place — it isn't that it retains 0% of a layer-0 velocity, it's that "layer-0 velocity" is not a quantity the architecture defines.

This is not an accident specific to GPT-2's trained weights — it is a structural property of the architecture family. The Conservative Obstruction argument (`The_Overdamped_Limit_and_The_Position_of_The_2nd_Order_Lagrangian_Framework.md`, §8; paper §15) gives six reasons a standard attention transformer cannot host a shared-potential second-order depth-axis dynamics even in principle: asymmetric $W_Q\neq W_K^\top$ (non-conservative force by construction), multi-head concatenation (no single shared potential), the causal mask (non-autonomous in token context), LayerNorm, distinct per-layer weights (non-autonomous in depth), and softmax's dissipative competition. `The_Failure_of_Conservative_Models_to_explain_hidden_state_trajectories.md` documents this being tried empirically — five conservative/Helmholtz-augmented fits to GPT-2's depth axis, none beating the static null. That negative result, not merely the absence of a $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$-style analysis, is why SPLM/Fock-PARFLM allocate the velocity coordinate *prescriptively* by design rather than attempting to discover one that a standard transformer never had.

**This sharpens how §4.1's "independent corroboration" claim should be read.** The Markov-order rejection test's Outcome C is a **position-as-time** (token-axis) result: it regresses $h_{t+1}^{(L)}$ against $h_t^{(L)}$ and $h_{t-1}^{(L)}$ at a fixed final layer $L$, holding depth fixed and varying token position. `first_order_ODE_rejection_pre-registered_protocol.md`'s own closing remarks are explicit that this pipeline does **not** test the Lu et al. layer-axis (depth) reading on GPT-2 at all, and flags exactly that test — regressing $h_t^{(\ell+1)}$ on $\{h_t^{(\ell)}, h_t^{(\ell-1)}\}$ at fixed token position $t$ — as an unexecuted follow-up. So the two pieces of evidence §4.1 juxtaposes are not two measurements of the same question by different methods; they are results about two different axes ($t$ vs. $\ell$) in two architecturally different systems — a transformer with no depth-axis velocity coordinate at all, versus Fock-PARFLM/SPLM, which has one by construction. Same-axis, same-architecture evidence for GPT-2's depth axis does not currently exist. The genuinely comparable missing experiment is not "run $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ on GPT-2's depth axis" (categorically unavailable, per the obstruction result above) but extending §3.1's cumulative-retention *methodology* to GPT-2's own *token* axis — asking whether token-1's contribution to the trajectory remains detectable by token-$T$ at the resolution a multi-step (not just lag-1) test could reveal. That is the same-axis analogue the current evidence base is missing, on the one object (a transformer) where such a test could actually be run.

**Conclusion of this section:** the corrected picture is more decisive about inference than an earlier draft of this section claimed, and no more decisive about training. Cumulatively (§3.1), $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ implies heavier long-range damping than any $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ ever tested — genuinely strong reinforcement of the inference-phase "observational first-order" reading (§4.1). But that reinforcement is silent on two separate things: (i) whether the surviving *short-range* momentum (§3.1's 2–3-layer retention) is doing real computational work a literal first-order recursion cannot replicate, and (ii) what would happen if the velocity state were removed at initialization and the network trained from scratch as a first-order system (§3.2–§3.3). Post-hoc approximability of a converged model's forward pass is not evidence that the smaller function class is equally *learnable* — the same reasoning that makes knowledge distillation work (a trained model is often compressible after the fact) does not imply training the compressed architecture from scratch reaches the same quality. That is a separate, structurally different question, addressed next.

## 4. Two Separate Hypotheses: Training-Phase vs Inference-Phase

The paper's position (v3, §12, "Generative second-order, observational first-order") already distinguishes two claims that are easy to conflate. This document keeps them explicitly separate, because the evidence for each is at a completely different stage of completion.

### 4.1 Inference-phase claim: is the *trained model's forward pass* well-approximated by a first-order ODE?

This is an **observational** claim about the fixed point reached after training, tested by the Markov-order regression protocol (`first_order_ODE_rejection_pre-registered_protocol.md`): does predicting $h_{t+1}$ from $h_t$ alone lose accuracy relative to using $\{h_t, h_{t-1}\}$? If not, the one-step (first-order) representation is not rejected.

- **Status: executed, 2026-04-27.** Outcome **C** — first-order **not rejected** on GPT-2-small ($\rho_{12}=0.979$, two-sided $p_{12}=0.124$) and confirmed on Pythia-160m.
- **Interpretation adopted by the paper:** this is the *predicted* observational signature of a second-order Lagrangian in the overdamped regime, not a refutation of it — at one-token temporal resolution, a heavily-damped second-order system and a genuinely first-order one are statistically indistinguishable by a Markov-order test.
- **How the $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ finding relates — and where the analogy stops (§3.4).** $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ is a qualitatively convergent, closed-form finding from a completely different methodology (Jacobi-metric geodesic fitting on Fock-PARFLM rather than Markov-order regression on GPT-2/Pythia), and per §3.1's cumulative-retention analysis it is a *quantitatively stronger* statement of near-first-order behaviour than the Markov-order test alone provides: compounded over the network's full depth, $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ implies that long-range velocity memory is negligible by the final layers, at every scale tested. But — as §3.4 makes precise — this is evidence about a different *axis* (depth, $\ell$) in a different *architecture* (one with an explicit depth-axis velocity coordinate by construction), not a second measurement of the same axis Outcome C tests (token position, $t$, at fixed depth). Standard transformers have no depth-axis velocity coordinate at all, so no analogous $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$-on-GPT-2 statement is even well-posed, and the paper's own protocol explicitly flags the one experiment that *would* be same-axis for GPT-2 — the layer-axis Markov-order test — as not yet run. Both pieces of evidence support "not rejected as approximately first-order at this resolution, on the axis each was actually measured on." Neither supports "is exactly first-order at every step," since (§3.1) short-range, few-layer momentum remains substantial even where long-range memory has decayed away — and neither licenses treating Fock-PARFLM's depth-axis finding as if it were a depth-axis finding about GPT-2.

### 4.2 Training-phase claim: does the inertial term contribute genuine value *during training*, at matched compute?

This is a **causal/architectural** claim: does removing the velocity buffer and the friction term (while keeping every other architectural component identical) and training a genuinely first-order model at the *same* learning-rate schedule produce a materially worse language model, or does a well-chosen but fixed step size fully recover the second-order winner's performance?

- **Design:** `SPLM-1_ablation_pre-registered_protocol.md`. SPLM-1 keeps $V_\theta$, the causal context pool $\xi_t$, the shared-$V_\psi$ structure, the optimiser, the LR schedule, and every other hyperparameter identical to the SPLM em_ln (second-order) baseline; the only change is removing the velocity buffer, the damping coefficient, and the inertial term. Deliberately, **no independent LR sweep is performed for SPLM-1** — it runs at the same nominal LR as the second-order winner, precisely so a defender of the null cannot claim an unfair pairing.
- **Hypotheses:** $H_1$ (genuine training-time value, $\overline\Delta \ge \Delta_{\min} > 0$ in PPL, matched compute) vs $H_0$ (the interior-$\gamma^\ast$ U-shape is an effective-learning-rate artifact and SPLM-1 matches it) vs $H_{-1}$ (SPLM-1 wins outright).
- **Status: executed on TinyShakespeare, in three stages, with $H_1$ ultimately confirmed.** (i) The original 3-seed sweep (`RESULTS.md`) found Outcome A at $\gamma=0.30$: $\overline\Delta=+23.18$ PPL, 3/3 sign-consistent, paired-$t$ (one-sided) $p=0.0048$ — but a later audit found the integrator had a causal-leak bug (`Causal_Leak_in_SPLM_Integrate_Bug_and_Fix.md`) that disproportionately inflated the second-order arm (2.07$\times$ vs. 1.65$\times$ for the first-order arm). Retrained leak-free at the same $\gamma=0.30$ (`RESULTS_LEAKFREE.md`), the gap collapsed 18$\times$ to $\overline\Delta=+1.27$ PPL, sign-mixed (2/3), paired-$t$ $p\approx0.42$, $d_z=0.58$ — not significant. (ii) A follow-up 4-point $\gamma$-sweep ($\gamma\in\{0.00,0.10,0.30,0.85\}$, $S=3$; `notebooks/conservative_arch/ln_damping_sweep/results/RESULTS_LEAKFREE_GAMMA_SWEEP.md`) found the actual PPL-optimal damping is $\gamma^\ast=0.10$ (mean val PPL $179.46\pm4.90$), not the $\gamma=0.30$ tested in (i) — the freely-trained value $\gamma\approx0.85$ was, in fact, suboptimal ($186.69\pm3.32$). Retesting SPLM-1 vs SPLM-2 at $\gamma^\ast=0.10$ gave $\overline\Delta_{0.10}=+4.71$ PPL, $3/3$ sign-consistent, paired-$t$ $p\approx0.107$, $d_z=+1.62$ — $0.29$ PPL short of the pre-registered $\Delta_{\min}=5.0$ at $S=3$. (iii) An $S=5$ confirmation sweep at $\gamma\in\{0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20\}$ (`notebooks/conservative_arch/ln_damping_sweep/results/leakfree_5seed_confirmation/RESULTS_CONFIRMATION_S5.md`) clears every pre-registered criterion at $\gamma=0.10$ ($\overline\Delta=+5.09$ PPL, $p=0.006$, $d_z=+2.37$, $5/5$) and at $\gamma=0.15$, the largest observed effect ($\overline\Delta=+7.03$ PPL, $p=0.013$, $d_z=+1.89$, $5/5$). **Verdict: Outcome A / $H_1$ confirmed.** Second-order architecturally lifts over the structurally first-order ablation under causally honest training, by $\sim$5–7 paired PPL across the $\gamma\in[0.10,0.15]$ basin (paper v5, §14 / E1 / E4–E5). The original $\gamma=0.30$ non-detection is now understood as an artefact of testing at a suboptimal $\gamma$, not as evidence against training-time inertial value — this is the key lesson also relevant to the Fock-PARFLM gamma-sweep-vs-full-training-run discrepancies discussed elsewhere in these companion notes. **Scope: this result covers only the original MLP-$V_\theta$ SPLM family on TinyShakespeare.** The analogous ablation for the anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$ + Fock-reg family this line of work actually uses (Fock-G1, §6) is in progress on TinyStories and has not yet been run on OpenWebText — neither has a completed result, so whether this TinyShakespeare $H_1$ confirmation generalises to this architecture family, or to larger scale and richer corpora, is still open.

### 4.3 The two claims can point in different directions without contradiction

Nothing prevents "the trained model looks nearly first-order at inference" (§4.1, evidence exists, consistent with a heavily-damped-but-not-fully-overdamped second-order system) from coexisting with "training-time inertia contributed real predictive value that a first-order model at matched LR could not have replicated" (§4.2 — a claim the leak-free TinyShakespeare retrain confirms at the PPL-optimal $\gamma^\ast\in[0.10,0.15]$ via an $S=5$ confirmation sweep, though whether it holds for the aniso-Gaussian/Fock-reg family this line of work actually uses is still open, §6). These are claims about different phases of the model's life cycle — one about the converged forward map, one about the optimization trajectory that produced it — and conflating them is the exact trap this document is trying to avoid.

The training-phase question (does the inertial term add genuine value during optimization?) is a structurally different claim: for the original MLP-$V_\theta$ SPLM family on TinyShakespeare it has been executed in three stages (§4.2) — an initial non-detection at $\gamma=0.30$, followed by a $\gamma$-sweep that located the PPL-optimal damping at $\gamma^\ast\in[0.10,0.15]$, where an $S=5$ confirmation sweep clears every pre-registered criterion for $H_1$ ($\overline\Delta=+5.09$ to $+7.03$ PPL, $p\le0.013$, $5/5$ sign-consistent). For the anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$ + Fock-reg family this line of work actually uses, no completed analogue exists yet — the Fock-G1 protocol (§6) is designed to fill that gap, and is in progress on TinyStories and not yet run on OpenWebText.

## 5. What the Fock-PARFLM Gamma Sweeps Do and Do Not Establish

The gamma sweeps are not the §4.2 ablation — they vary $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ within the second-order family, they do not compare against a structurally first-order model. But they do bound the question, and the bound is scale-dependent.

| Sweep | $\gamma^\ast$ | Shape | What it establishes about $H_1$ vs $H_0$ |
|---|---:|---|---|
| d=256 aniso-Gaussian (full 20K run) | 0.300 | **Interior minimum** (worse at 0.05 *and* at 0.50) | Some intermediate friction beats both extremes *within the second-order family* — consistent with, but (per §3.3) not dispositive proof of, $H_1$ |
| d=384 MLP | 0.250 | **Interior minimum** (483.81 → 342.02 → 740.62) | Same as above; largest relative swing of any Fock-PARFLM sweep |
| d=384 aniso-Gaussian | 0.100 | **Interior minimum** (632.03 → 278.27 → 596.87) | Same as above |
| d=768 MLP (old) | 0.050 | **Boundary optimum** — sweep floor wins, clean monotonic decrease | Only shows "lower beats higher within [0.05, 0.50]"; does not rule out $\gamma \to 0$ being even better |
| d=768 aniso-Gaussian (new) | 0.050 | **Boundary optimum** — sweep floor wins, but non-monotonic beyond it (local peak at 0.20, partial recovery at 0.50) | Same as above |
| d=1024 isotropic Gaussian | 0.050 | **Boundary optimum** — sweep floor wins (4/8 candidates tested) | Same as above |
| d=1024 aniso-Gaussian (new) | 0.050 | **Boundary optimum** — sweep floor wins by the widest margin of any $d\ge768$ sweep (~10–11%), same non-monotonic wiggle as d=768 aniso | Same as above |

Two things follow from this table:

1. **A genuine interior minimum — the strongest available signature of $H_1$-consistent behaviour — has only been observed at $d \le 384$.** At $d \ge 768$, every sweep run so far (now four, across MLP, isotropic Gaussian, and anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$) has never found a lower boundary; the true optimum could be at $\gamma_{\text{train}} \to 0$ (pure conservative/Hamiltonian dynamics, no explicit friction at all) rather than at some interior value. The two new aniso-Gaussian sweeps add a further wrinkle without changing this conclusion: both are non-monotonic *past* $\gamma=0.05$ (a local peak in the $\gamma \in [0.20, 0.40]$ range, then partial recovery toward $\gamma=0.50$), yet in neither case does the recovery come close to beating $\gamma=0.05$. This is not an interior minimum — the global optimum is still the swept boundary — but it is a shape the MLP and isotropic-Gaussian sweeps never showed, and it is currently unresolved whether it reflects single-seed noise or a reproducible property of the anisotropic-precision architecture (`Determining_optimal_gamma_for_Fock-PARFLM.md` §14.5).
2. **Even the cleanest interior minima (d=256, d=384) do not by themselves discriminate $H_1$ from $H_0$.** A pure first-order model swept over its own step size would *also* typically show a U-shape — too small a step underfits, too large destabilizes. Shape alone is generic optimization behaviour, not unique evidence that inertia specifically is doing useful work. Discriminating the two requires the matched-compute, fixed-LR, structurally-different-architecture comparison that only §4.2's design (or its analogue below) provides.

## 6. Protocol and First Result: Fock-G1, the Aniso-Gaussian First-Order Ablation

`SPLM-1_ablation_pre-registered_protocol.md` answers the training-phase question for the original MLP-$V_\theta$ SPLM. There is currently **no analogous ablation for the anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$ + Fock coupling regularisation family** — the architecture that every scale-up sweep from d=256 through the pending d=1024 run actually uses. This section drafts that protocol, following the same structure and the same fixed-LR design discipline as the SPLM-1 original. **This is a draft for review, not a locked pre-registration** — exact implementation details should be checked against the live `model_parf_multixi.py` / `model_fock_parf_v2.py` code before any commit-hash lock-in.

### 6.1 The Fock-G1 model

The only architectural change is in the position-update line of `_layer_step`; every other component — the aniso-Gaussian $V_\theta$, the pairwise $V_\phi$ (structural_competitive), the multi-channel $\xi$ context, the Fock register creation/destruction gates, the reverse channel, the Fock coupling regularisation term, `force_clamp_max` — is identical between the two arms.

| Family | Force | Position update |
|---|---|---|
| Fock v2.1 aniso-Gaussian (second-order, current sweeps) | same as Fock-G1 (identical) | full damped-Verlet step, Eq. in §2 |
| **Fock-G1** (first-order, proposed) | same as second-order arm (identical) | pure gradient step, Eq. below |

Both arms compute the identical force from the identical potentials:

$$
f = -\nabla_h\big(V_\theta + U_{\text{pair}}\big).
$$

The two arms differ only in how that force updates the position. The second-order arm uses the full damped-Verlet step already given in §2. Fock-G1 replaces it with a pure gradient step, dropping the velocity memory term and the friction coefficient entirely:

$$
h_{\text{new}} = \mathrm{LN}\big(h_{\text{old}} + \beta f\big).
$$

Fock-G1 **drops**: the velocity/displacement memory term $\delta_\ell/(1+\Delta t \gamma)$ entirely — and with it any *dynamical* role for the friction coefficient $\gamma$ (see the clarification at the end of this subsection).

Fock-G1 **retains**: everything that makes the model a Fock-PARFLM rather than a generic scalar-potential network — the pairwise $V_\phi$ force is summed into $f_\ell$ exactly as in the second-order arm, so this ablation isolates the inertial term specifically, not the broader Fock coupling machinery.

**The step-size constant $\beta$**, to keep the comparison at matched LR (no independent tuning advantage for either arm, per the SPLM-1 design discipline), is fixed — not learned — at

$$
\beta = \frac{\Delta t^2}{\bar m (1+\Delta t \gamma^\ast)},
$$

i.e. exactly the initial effective step size the second-order anchor arm uses at its own sweep-optimal $\gamma^\ast$, with $\bar m$ the mean semantic mass. This makes Fock-G1's *initial* per-step displacement magnitude identical to the anchor's; if $H_0$ is correct (the interior optimum is purely an effective-step-size artifact), this choice of $\beta$ is exactly the one that should let Fock-G1 match the anchor.

> **Important clarification — $\gamma^\ast$ in Fock-G1 is not a damping coefficient.** Because $\gamma^\ast$ reappears inside $\beta$, it is easy to read Eq. above as "a first-order model that still has friction." It does not. Damping, by definition, is the coefficient multiplying a *velocity* term (the $-m\gamma\dot h$ in $m\ddot h = -\nabla V - m\gamma\dot h$); it can only act if there is a velocity. Fock-G1 sets the velocity memory $\delta_\ell := 0$ at every layer, so there is nothing for a friction term to act on. What survives is a **pure first-order gradient flow** $h_{\text{new}} = \mathrm{LN}(h_{\text{old}} + \beta f)$ in which $\gamma^\ast$ is nothing more than a *frozen constant that pins the value of the single step-size knob* $\beta$. It never multiplies a velocity and exerts no smoothing across layers. First-order-vs-second-order is a statement about whether an inertial (velocity) term exists — Fock-G1 has none — not about whether the symbol $\gamma$ appears in a step-size formula.
>
> **How this differs from the SPLM-1 (TinyShakespeare) convention.** SPLM-1 (`first_order_ablation/model_first_order.py`) makes the stronger syntactic choice of setting $\gamma = 0$ outright (a non-trainable buffer, "no damping coefficient") and steps with $h_{\text{new}} = \mathrm{LN}(h + \Delta t f/m)$ — $\gamma$ absent from the update entirely, with the step size taken as the plain $\Delta t/m$. Fock-G1 instead keeps $\gamma^\ast$ inside $\beta$ **on purpose**, so that its per-layer step equals the second-order anchor's *layer-0* effective step $\Delta t^2/(m(1+\Delta t\gamma^\ast))$ exactly. This is the matched-step-size design of §6.3: it neutralises the §3.3 confound (a higher sweep-$\gamma$ mechanically shrinks the effective step), whereas SPLM-1 used $\Delta t/m$ — actually $\sim(1+\Delta t\gamma^\ast)$ times *larger* than its own second-order baseline's initial step — and controlled fairness only through the shared nominal learning rate, not through matched step size. Both models are therefore genuinely first-order; they differ solely in how the single step-size knob is pinned, and Fock-G1's matched-$\beta$ is the tighter control for this particular comparison. If strict syntactic consistency with the SPLM-1 precedent were preferred instead, one would set $\gamma=0$ and $\beta=\Delta t/m$; we deliberately do not, for the confound-control reason above.

### 6.2 The comparison anchor

**Primary arm: $d=384$, anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$, $\gamma^\ast = 0.100$** (PPL 278.27, the sweep winner from §12 of `Determining_optimal_gamma_for_Fock-PARFLM.md`). This scale is chosen because it has the cleanest, largest-effect-size interior minimum of any completed aniso-Gaussian sweep (632.03 at $\gamma=0.05$ down to 278.27 at $\gamma=0.10$, back up to 596.87 at $\gamma=0.50$ — a 2.3× range, larger than either the d=256 aniso sweep or the original SPLM E5 sweep), giving the ablation the best chance of a clearly-resolvable signal.

**Secondary arm (optional, recommended if compute allows): $d=768$ or $d=1024$, $\gamma^\ast=0.050$.** Because neither scale has ever shown an interior minimum in its own sweep (§5 — now confirmed independently at $d=768$ *and* $d=1024$ with the aniso-Gaussian $V_\theta$, in addition to the earlier MLP/isotropic results), a Fock-G1 comparison here answers a *different* and arguably more interesting question: whether the boundary-optimum, low-$\gamma$ preference at large $d$ reflects a genuine training-time need for inertia (in which case Fock-G1 should underperform there too), or whether at large $d$ the model's task-relevant behaviour is closer to $H_0$ even though it wasn't at $d=384$ (in which case the $H_1$/$H_0$ answer would itself be $d$-dependent — a new and notable finding in its own right, echoing the already-documented $d$-dependent phase transition in the *damping regime*, §3.5 of `Fock-PARFLM_Scale-Up_Gamma_Sweep_Results_and_Damping_Regime_Analysis.md`). Of the two, $d=1024$ is the marginally better choice if only one can be run: its boundary optimum has the widest margin (~10–11%) of any sweep in §5's table, so a Fock-G1 result close to the second-order anchor there would be the least confounded by "the sweep just couldn't tell" — the reading that has to be ruled out first before the $H_1$/$H_0$ answer at large $d$ can be trusted.

### 6.3 Controlling the §3.3 confound

Because §3.3 identified that the existing $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ sweeps confound "more friction" with "smaller effective step," this protocol's fixed-$\beta$ design (§6.1) is specifically constructed to *not* repeat that confound in the ablation itself: Fock-G1 gets exactly the anchor's own initial effective step size, not a step size that happens to shrink as some other knob is turned. If Fock-G1 still underperforms at this matched step size, "the first-order reduction was just mistuned" is no longer an available explanation.

### 6.4 Hypotheses and decision rule

Let $P_A^{(s)}$ be Fock-G1's final validation PPL at seed $s$, $P_B^{(s)}$ the second-order anchor's, with $S=3$ seeds and $\overline\Delta = \frac{1}{S}\sum_s (P_A^{(s)} - P_B^{(s)})$.

| Hypothesis | Operational form | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| $H_1$ (training-time value) | $\overline\Delta \ge \Delta_{\min} > 0$ | inertia contributes genuine value beyond any first-order reduction at matched compute and matched step size |
| $H_0$ (artefact) | $\lvert\overline\Delta\rvert < \Delta_{\min}$ | the interior $\gamma^\ast$ is an effective-step-size artefact; Fock-G1 matches |
| $H_{-1}$ (refutation) | $\overline\Delta \le -\Delta_{\min}$ | Fock-G1 outperforms; the training-time-value claim is falsified for this architecture family |

**Proposed $\Delta_{\min} = 15.0$ PPL units** for the $d=384$ primary arm. Justification, mirroring the SPLM-1 protocol's own logic (§5 of that document, which used ~4–5-point adjacent-cell gaps at PPL~87–94): the adjacent-in-the-bowl gaps at $d=384$ aniso-Gaussian are $283.35 - 278.27 = 5.08$ ($\gamma=0.10 \to 0.15$) and $292.37 - 278.27 = 14.10$ ($\gamma=0.10 \to 0.25$); at this PPL scale (~278 vs. SPLM's ~87, roughly 3.2×), a proportionally-scaled version of the SPLM-1 threshold ($5.0 \times 3.2 \approx 16$) lands close to the larger of the two observed interior gaps. $\Delta_{\min}=15.0$ is proposed as a round number consistent with both approaches; a genuine training-time-value effect should be at least as large as the gap between adjacent points *inside* the already-observed bowl, or the mechanism is contributing less than a single $\gamma$-grid step of value.

Same decision-rule structure as SPLM-1 (§5 of that protocol): Outcome A requires $\overline\Delta \ge \Delta_{\min}$, consistent sign across all three seeds, and a paired one-sided Wilcoxon test at $p \le 0.10$.

### 6.5 What this would and would not settle

Confirming $H_1$ at $d=384$ would **not** automatically extend to $d\ge768$, given §5's finding that the interior-minimum signature itself has only been observed at $d\le384$ so far. This is exactly why §6.2 recommends the $d=768$ secondary arm: the training-phase question may itself be $d$-dependent, symmetric to how the optimal damping *regime* already is.

### 6.6 Completed instance — TinyStories at d=256 (single seed, executed August 16, 2026)

The first instantiation of this protocol has now been run — not at the §6.2 primary $d=384$ anchor, but at the cheaper $d=256$ TinyStories scale, anchored against that sweep's own interior minimum ($\gamma^\ast=0.30$, the full-20K aniso-Gaussian run from §5's table). Everything else follows §6.1: identical aniso-Gaussian $V_\theta$ + Fock-reg architecture (4 context channels, 8 wells/channel, low-rank $r=4$, $L=8$, 26.6M params), matched-$\beta$ first-order step, only the single position-update line changed. $\Delta_{\min}$ is set to **1.0 PPL** for this corpus (adjacent-cell gaps at this ~9 PPL scale are sub-point; 1.0 is a conservative round threshold, roughly one grid step of value).

**Perplexity — $H_0$.** Trained to 20,000 steps under the causal probe (passed, $\max\Delta=0$ — the first-order variant remains causally honest), Fock-G1 reaches best validation PPL **8.95**, against the second-order anchor's **9.04** at the same $\gamma^\ast=0.30$: $\overline\Delta = -0.09$ PPL, well inside $\Delta_{\min}=1.0$. For orientation on the same corpus: MLP-$V_\theta$ 9.70, SQ3 quadratic 10.90, isotropic Gaussian without Fock-reg 16.33. At single-seed resolution the reading is **$H_0$** — the first-order flow matches, and nominally edges, the second-order arm; the $d=256$ interior-$\gamma$ minimum behaves as a step-size effect, not as training-time evidence for inertia. *Caveats:* single seed (the full $S=3$ protocol and a paired Wilcoxon test are still required before this is locked); and the run showed none of the `Training_Instabilities...` §23–25 late-training gradient spikes, but at $L=8$/20K steps it never reaches the width/duration regime where those appear — so this is absence of counter-evidence, not evidence of superior first-order stability.

**The learned potential is preserved, not only the perplexity.** Matching PPL leaves open the sharper question: does second-order training sculpt a *different* potential that merely scores the same? This matters because the Jacobi metric and its geodesics (paper v5 §Riemannian) are a functional of $V_\theta$ alone, not of the integrator that traverses it. We compared the two learned aniso-Gaussian potentials directly — centres $\mu$, diagonal precisions $a$, low-rank factors $B$, mixture weights $w$, per-layer depth codes — on a shared probe-context bank, using a same-order baseline as the noise floor: two second-order runs differing only in damping ($\gamma=0.30$ vs $\gamma=0.15$). On every axis the first-order-vs-second-order divergence is no larger than — and mostly smaller than — that same-order $\gamma$ baseline:

| Axis | FO vs SO ($\gamma=0.30$) | SO vs SO ($\gamma=0.30$ vs $0.15$) |
|---|---:|---:|
| mean $\lambda_{\max}$ %diff | +7.9% | +10.8% |
| mean trace %diff | +5.6% | +19.9% |
| mean anisotropy %diff | +10.8% | +19.8% |
| depth-code cosine sim | 0.98 | 0.93 |
| precision-bias $a$ cosine sim | >0.999 | >0.999 |
| $\mu$, $a$-weight cosine sim | 0.33–0.53 | 0.35–0.63 |
| $w$, $B$ cosine sim (fine routing) | 0.09–0.14 | 0.05–0.25 |

The pattern is structured and identical across both comparisons: the *global-scale* parameters (per-layer depth conditioning, baseline well precision) converge almost exactly regardless of training order or $\gamma$, while the *fine well-routing* parameters ($w$, $B$) are only weakly correlated — but *equally* weakly correlated between the two same-order runs, marking this as an intrinsic degeneracy of the over-parameterised mixture, not a fingerprint of the training order. **Conclusion: on TinyStories, second-order training leaves no detectable imprint on the geodesic-defining potential beyond what changing $\gamma$ alone produces.** (Scripts and full tables: `notebooks/conservative_arch/first_order_ablation/scripts/compare_vtheta_profiles.py`; reports `fock_g1_vs_second_order_report.json`, `same_order_baseline_report.json`.)

### 6.7 Consequence — train-cheap / infer-geometric, and the criterion it still needs

Two facts now sit together on TinyStories: (i) first-order training reproduces second-order perplexity, and (ii) it reproduces the same $V_\theta$, hence the same Jacobi metric and geodesics. Because the inference-time geometric amenities — hallucination detection, geodesic analogical reasoning, curvature-as-uncertainty, the energy-dissipation anomaly — are read off the potential and then *traversed* by the second-order CfC/BAOAB integrator, and because that integrator is a deterministic function of $V_\theta$ and a chosen $\gamma$ rather than a trained artefact, a decomposition follows on any corpus where $H_0$ holds: **train under first-order dynamics, deploy under second-order inference.** This answers directly the train-first/infer-second question raised in `Replacing_The_Conservative_Mechanism_of_SPLM_with_First_Order.md`: the objection that first-order training might never sculpt an inference-ready potential is refuted on TinyStories — the potential is indistinguishable from the second-order one — leaving only the inference-time $\gamma$ calibration, which the four-estimator $\gamma^\ast$ predictor (`Determining_optimal_gamma_for_Fock-PARFLM.md`) already supplies without any trained velocity channel.

The decomposition is only as safe as the $H_0$ that licenses it, and $H_0$ is **not universal**.

**A sensitivity budget: order, damping, and width (executed August 16, 2026).** To place the training-order null in context, we extended the same $V_\theta$ diagnostic to a matched $d\times\gamma$ grid over the second-order OpenWebText sweeps — $d\in\{384,768,1024\}\times\gamma\in\{0.05,0.10,0.30\}$, all at $L=16$, aniso-rank 4, 3,000 steps — and ranked the three known perturbations of the learned potential by how much each moves its (dimensionless) anisotropy ratio $\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$:

| $d\backslash\gamma$ | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.30 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 384 | 5.17e4 | 5.91e4 | 5.74e4 |
| 768 | 2.19e5 | 2.23e5 | 1.89e5 |
| 1024 | 2.31e5 | 2.41e5 | 2.30e5 |

*(mean anisotropy ratio; full grid including max/worst-case, entropy fraction, $\lambda_{\max}$, trace, and depth-code-variation stats saved in `vtheta_grid_report.json`.)*

Relative spread of mean anisotropy: **across $\gamma$ at fixed $d$** — 14.3% ($d{=}384$), 18.0% ($d{=}768$), 4.6% ($d{=}1024$); **across $d$ at fixed $\gamma$** — 346.0% ($\gamma{=}0.05$), 307.1% ($\gamma{=}0.10$), 300.6% ($\gamma{=}0.30$). So: damping, within a fixed $d$, moves the potential by ~5–18% — the same small order of magnitude as the ~6–11% training-order effect measured at $d=256$ (§6.6). Width, within a fixed $\gamma$, moves it by ~300–346% (a ~4× range from $d=384$ to $d=1024$) — **one to two orders of magnitude larger than either order or damping.** The pattern also saturates: most of the width-driven shift happens by $d=768$, with $d=768\to1024$ adding comparatively little at any of the three $\gamma$'s tested (consistent with $L=16$ being the depth-reduction mitigation of `Training_Instabilities_in_Fock-PARFLM_with_structured_V_theta.md` §23–25, and with the earlier single-$\gamma$ observation that the worst-case tail eases past $d=768$ at this depth). Order and damping are soft, comparably-sized perturbations; width is the dominant one. A corpus or scale where the inertial term *does* carry training-time value — a genuine interior $\gamma^\ast$ that is not a step-size artefact — would break the order-damping equivalence, and the recipe would then train the wrong potential.

**The open contribution.** What is missing is a *predictive criterion*, computable from corpus and architecture properties before running any ablation, for when $H_0$ holds — i.e. when training can be reduced to first order with no loss to the learned potential, and when it cannot. Candidate ingredients, all already instrumented in this note's apparatus: the sweep shape (interior minimum vs boundary optimum, §5); the gap between $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ and $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ (§2); the two-knob degrees-of-freedom collapse (§3.2); and the width-driven curvature growth just measured. Turning these into a single a-priori test would upgrade the train-cheap/infer-geometric decomposition from a per-corpus empirical finding into a **design rule** — a genuine theoretical contribution. Until then it is warranted only where Fock-G1 has been run and returned $H_0$ — at present, TinyStories at $d=256$. The OWT / $d\ge384$ instances (§6.2) are the next test of how far it reaches; this section will be updated in place when they land.

## 7. Summary

- **Two damping channels exist and are separable.** The explicit $\gamma_{\text{train}}$ dial and the implicit damping contributed by LayerNorm and potential curvature are different quantities; the latter dominates, converging to $\gamma_{\text{geo}}\approx0.9$–$0.98$ ($r_{\text{layer}}\approx0.51$ per layer) across every scale, corpus, and $V_\theta$ family tested to date.
- **Cumulatively, $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ implies a heavily overdamped regime — but this still does not imply a first-order reduction would match second-order performance.** By the identical full-stack retention convention the sweep-results doc uses to label regimes, $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$ implies roughly 1,000x more long-range damping than the explicitly-labeled-overdamped d=384 configuration, at every scale (§3.1) — genuinely strong reinforcement of the inference-phase claim (§4.1). But short-range, 2–3-layer momentum remains substantial even so; the first-order reduction still collapses two independently-tunable quantities into one (§3.2); and the existing sweeps still confound higher $\gamma$ with smaller effective step size (§3.3) — none of which bears on whether training as first-order from scratch would match (§4.2).
- **The compounding picture is specific to architectures with an explicit depth-axis velocity coordinate (§3.4).** Standard attention transformers, under the paper's own Lu-et-al. layer-as-time first-order reading, have phase-space dimension $n$ (not $2n$) on the depth axis — no velocity coordinate exists there to compound in the first place, for six structural reasons the Conservative Obstruction argument catalogues. Fock-PARFLM/SPLM's $\approx99.998\%$ cumulative long-range damping is therefore not directly comparable to "how overdamped is GPT-2's depth axis," a question the architecture does not even pose. The Markov-order test's Outcome C on GPT-2 is a *token-position*-axis result at fixed depth, not a depth-axis result — the paper's own protocol explicitly flags the depth-axis (layer-axis) version of that test on GPT-2 as an unexecuted follow-up, making it the genuinely comparable missing experiment, not $\gamma_{\text{geo}}$-on-GPT-2.
- **Training-phase and inference-phase are separate claims with separate, differently-resourced evidence.** The inference-phase claim (first-order not rejected at the trained fixed point) is **settled** (Outcome C, executed 2026-04-27, on GPT-2/Pythia). The training-phase claim (inertia adds genuine value during optimization) has been **executed and confirmed for the original MLP-$V_\theta$ SPLM family on TinyShakespeare** (`SPLM-1_ablation_pre-registered_protocol.md`; the initial $\gamma=0.30$ leak-free retrain was a non-detection, but a follow-up $\gamma$-sweep located the PPL-optimal damping at $\gamma^\ast\in[0.10,0.15]$, where an $S=5$ confirmation sweep clears every pre-registered criterion for $H_1$, $\overline\Delta=+5.09$ to $+7.03$ PPL — §4.2), and, **as of August 16, 2026, has a first completed analogue for the anisotropic Gaussian $V_\theta$ + Fock-reg family** — a single-seed Fock-G1 run on TinyStories at $d=256$ (§6.6) that returns **$H_0$**: first-order matches second-order in PPL (8.95 vs 9.04, $\overline\Delta=-0.09$, $\Delta_{\min}=1.0$) *and* leaves the learned $V_\theta$ potential structurally indistinguishable from the second-order one (no imprint beyond the same-order $\gamma$ noise floor). The full $S=3$ protocol and the OpenWebText / $d\ge384$ instances remain future work.
- **The Fock-PARFLM gamma sweeps bound but do not resolve the training-phase question, and the bound is itself scale-dependent.** Genuine interior minima (the strongest available $H_1$-consistent signature within the second-order family) appear only at $d\le384$; at $d\ge768$, four independent sweeps now (MLP, isotropic Gaussian, and — as of August 14, 2026 — aniso-Gaussian at both $d=768$ and $d=1024$) have all hit their lower boundary without turning back up, though the two new aniso-Gaussian sweeps add a not-yet-explained non-monotonic wiggle *past* $\gamma=0.05$ that never overturns it (§5).
- **§6 specifies Fock-G1** — a fixed-step-size, matched-compute first-order ablation of the aniso-Gaussian architecture — and now reports its first executed instance (§6.6, TinyStories $d=256$, $H_0$). The still-pending primary $d=384$ arm ($\gamma^\ast=0.10$) and the $d=768$/$d=1024$ secondary arm (the latter better-motivated by its wider ~10–11% boundary-optimum margin) are the concrete next experiments for the architecture family this line of work is built on.
- **A train-cheap / infer-geometric decomposition, and the open criterion it needs (§6.7).** Because the geodesic-defining object is $V_\theta$ alone, and on TinyStories first- and second-order training converge to the *same* $V_\theta$, one can **train first-order and run second-order inference** wherever $H_0$ holds — keeping the simpler single-knob training flow while forfeiting none of the Riemannian inference amenities (the second-order integrator is a deterministic function of $V_\theta$ and a chosen $\gamma$, not a trained artefact). This holds only where $H_0$ is established: a matched $d\times\gamma$ sensitivity-budget grid (§6.7) shows training order and damping are comparably small perturbations of the potential (~6–18%), while width alone moves it by ~300–346% — one to two orders of magnitude more — so it does not auto-extend to OWT/large $d$. The missing — and genuinely significant — theoretical contribution is a **predictive criterion**, from corpus/architecture properties before any ablation, for when $H_0$ holds.
